## Where the risk actually is
- **Money-first ordering** is the load-bearing decision: the provider refund is issued BEFORE the
  aggregate mutates, so a gateway failure leaves a paid order paid rather than a cancelled order
  whose money never came back. Test 11 pins it; the reverse ordering would be silently wrong in
  production and invisible in a green suite.
- **The `LogCritical` between the two steps** (money moved, record refused) is deliberate: if that
  branch ever fires, ops must reconcile by hand. Copied from the reviewed T-0105 shape.
- **Idempotency is asserted with observable effects**, not just a status: the re-run tests check
  that `RefundedAmountMinor` does not double and that no second outbox row is enqueued.
- **The window is config, not a constant**, and one test moves the row to prove the boundary
  follows it — otherwise "it's configurable" is an unverified claim.

## Correction I made to my own ticket
I wrote "no state-machine change needed" during grooming; that was wrong. A full `Refund()` ends in
`Refunded`, but the user asked for the order to be **cancelled**, and both cannot run. I added one
aggregate method (`RefuseByMaker`) that records the money and stamps the cancellation as a single
transition, per CLAUDE.md §2.2, and said so in the test plan rather than letting the ticket keep
claiming otherwise.

## Scope discipline
The customer's cancel is `PendingPayment`-only and the handler enforces that even though the
aggregate would accept `Paid` — the role rule is a command-layer concern and the test asserts the
refusal. That boundary is the whole reason a customer-facing refund on made-to-order goods cannot
happen by accident.

## Other dimensions

the IDOR shield on both hosts; no PII in the log lines (ids and amounts only); i18n in one block.
